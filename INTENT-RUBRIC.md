# Mentaat — Intent & Success Rubric
*The standard every page is designed against and judged against. Removes "do I like it" in favor of "did the page do its one job." Generated from the site-intent brainstorm. House style: no em dashes.*

## Parent intent (the belief the whole site must create)
> **"They've solved this exact problem for a business like mine."**

Proof + relevance lead. Every page's job is to connect *this owner's situation* to *evidence
Mentaat has done it.* Recognition is **layered**: the homepage greets by **pain/outcome** (broad
front door, any owner sees themselves); **industry pages** give deep vertical proof (the rooms).

## How success is measured (the Signal column)
**Two layers:**
1. **Design-time (usable now, pre-launch): the 10-second skim test.** A target boutique owner
   skims the page ~10s and can answer: *What does Mentaat do? Is this for a business like mine?
   What's my next step?* and has seen **one piece of relevant proof.** Pass/fail. This is what
   the adversarial critics score against.
2. **Live (post-launch): the conversion metric** for that page (book-a-call clicks, etc.).

**Not every page's outcome is "book now."** Each page's real next step is explicit below.

## Voice (applies to all copy)
Operator to owner. Short sentences. Plain verbs. Their words for their business, not our tech.
Specific beats clever (a real metric beats any adjective). No jargon. No em dashes.

## What "truly mine" means (the differentiation test)
A page fails the differentiation test if it could belong to any competent agency. It passes when
it is unmistakably Mentaat: **real, named-by-situation proof** + the **forward-deployed "we go in
and build" ethos** + the plainspoken operator voice. The critics check this explicitly.
